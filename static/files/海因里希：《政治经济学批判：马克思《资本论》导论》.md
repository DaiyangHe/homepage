海因里希是新马克思阅读的代表人物。这一思想运动从巴克豪斯开始，脱胎于法兰克福学派。阿多诺曾称巴克豪斯的工作是法兰克福学派最重要的工作之一。每每谈到法兰克福学派，我们总是想起一些晦涩的文化批判而缺少政治经济学内容。新马克思阅读则补充了这一点，他们以《资本论》为核心，展示了法兰克福学派“政经”的位面。

海因里希首先谈论价值。海因里希的一大重要观点是反对对价值的实体主义理解。马克思在资本论里形容价值时，称其为凝结在商品中的无差别的人类劳动，由社会必要劳动时间来衡量。在这里，价值和使用价值似乎存在着某种康德式的二分。价值仿佛是一种实体，自在地被人类劳动凝结在商品中。问题在于**商品并不生来是商品，只有当被放入市场交换时，他才成为商品。那么商品的价值也就绝非是在生产时就确定，而是在市场中的交换行为中确定的**。这里海因里希看似将价值解释为一种纯粹理念，其实不然。商品被生产出来，只有被总的社会化的大生产所承认，也就是进入市场交换，谈论其价值才有意义。这里的问题在于**私人劳动和社会大生产的割裂**。私人劳动生产的产品积压起来是没有价值的，只有进行交换才有，而交换时被赋予的价值就是私人劳动被社会总劳动承认的部分。海因里希也因此展开了对古典政治经济学中劳动价值论的批判。李嘉图是从经济上的自我异化，从商品被二重化为价值物(假想物)和现实物的这一事实出发的。他致力于把劳动作为价值的基础。他没注意到，在做完这一工作之后，主要的事情还没有做呢。因为，商品使自己和自己本身分离，并使自己转入一个独立的经济范畴王国，而固定为意识，这一事实，只能用社会劳动的自我分裂和自我矛盾来说明。而货币就是在这个交换过程中产生的为交换而生的商品，“商品之王”。

在资本论中，货币的形成经历了从个别的价值形式，扩大的等价形式到一般等价形式的变化。海因里希认为货币最重要的职能是价值尺度。因为在充当价值尺度时，货币作为持续存在参与了资本增值的每一个过程，也即著名的“G-W-G”的公式过程。海因里希认为，**拜物教与其说是对交换与生产关系的误认，不如说是正确认识到了商品交换的中介地位：在商品生产的条件下，生产者不是直接地、社会地联系起来，而是以他们的劳动产品为中介。人与人的关系实质上就表现为物与物的关系。在这个关系中，个体的理性服从于体系总的非理性**。海因里希对此提出的解决方案非常简单：消灭资本主义。计划经济体制下社会化大生产直接分配私人劳动，撤销掉了交换的中介，也就斩除了拜物教的根基。

这一部分里海因里希（或者说整个新马克思阅读）有一个致命的问题：**过于注重逻辑的严密而忽略了历史性地分析**。整个新马克思阅读的分析是共时性的，它只能说明资本主义的简单生产像资本增值转换的逻辑必然性，而不能说明转换的条件和历史。我们知道资本主义的简单生产“W-G-W”并不是一瞬间就向资本增值“G-W-G”转化的。以农民为例，在封建时代农民的生产首先是为了自己使用，其次是为了上税，其中并没有资本主义增值的逻辑：商品交换的逻辑，其进行的是商品的简单生产。而只有在资本主义时代，农业工人才以交换为目的生产。这其中的区别是，在封建时代，农民因为他是农民，所以才生产；而在资本主义时代，他是需要进行商品交换才选择成为农民。我们可以看出，商品的简单生产与资本增值并非共时性的，而是依托一定历史条件存在、转化的。

接下来，海因里希批判了传统马克思主义者对马克思剥削概念的解读。传统马克思主义者会认为剥削是不道德的，而海因里希认为剥削不是“不道德”，而是“**非道德**”。如果我们将劳动力比作一管牙膏，资本家支付工资实际上是支付了劳动力的价值。而我们又知道，价值和使用价值不是一回事，对资本家来说，买下了这管牙膏，他的当然要挤得干干净净。他不仅不会觉得挤牙膏不道德，他还会指责这管牙膏设计不好，不能挤得更干净。这是符合资产阶级法权道德的。但是无产者同样有话说，你看工人过的多惨啊，怎么能这样挤人呢？因此，这里出现了二律背反。在这里，道德不起作用，因而是非道德的。当道德失效，力量就起了决定性作用。因此决定工作日时长的就是工人与资本家的斗争形势。

海因里希同样对剩余价值概念的传统解读提出反对。当代学界有一些学者认为，可以不要超额剩余价值，只要绝对、相对剩余价值，或者反过来。海因里希以无主体过程反对这一点。在马克思资本论中，资本家是为了求取相对剩余价值，才提高生产力，榨取更多剩余价值。其实仔细想想，资本家真的有这么聪明吗？或者说，**只有熟悉剩余价值理论的资本家才会追求生产力提高吗**？海因里希认为，相对剩余价值只是“拉平”后的超额剩余价值。使得资本家追求科技进步生产力提高的是强制性的竞争，他必须提高生产力，负责会因为得不到超额剩余价值而倒闭。这里是一个无主体过程，结构自身的运转会给出其下一阶段。我们不关心资本家看没看透这层逻辑，无论如何他都得跟着结构的运转走，除非他不想当资本家了。这也就是引出另一个论断：**不能认为资本家具有独立的人格**。主体仅仅是结构运转结果的承受者，资本体系的实存需要依靠他们的中介。

最后，我们要讨论另一个政治意义和学术意义都非常重大的问题：诸如服务工作、家务家政算不算劳动？算不算雇佣劳动？算不算生产性的劳动？海因里希认为，这些劳动不产生价值。这也是海因里希与女性主义马克思主义者的矛盾。海因里希在这里的论断其实有点扯，有些由果推因。他认为因为无产者必须穷的只剩下自由才会进入资本主义生产体系，而如果家务劳动能产生价值，那他就不是穷得只剩自由了，他就不必进入资本主义生产体系了，因此家务不产生价值。而他的前提”无产者必须穷的只剩下自由才会进入资本主义生产体系“在马克思的时代是完全成立的，而现在则不然。我们可以看到，工人进入资本主义生产体系往往并非走投无路，比如大学毕业生进厂打螺丝挣得不一定比自己搞直播多。一些上世纪初发展起来的老牌资本主义国家也会提倡劳动光荣的观点，教育年轻人进入工厂成为雇佣工人。或者在一些福利国家，年轻人长时间失业许多福利会被取消，如果你不是雇佣工人或者说合格劳动力就不承认你是人。国家机器有许多方法迫使无产者进入资本主义生产体系，因此这个前提是不必要的。事实情况是**家务劳动是在进行劳动力的再生产。这一部分不能被放入商品交换的市场上，因为劳动力再生产成本很高，资本不可能承受得了（至少在现在是这样）。因此这部分必须要让家庭中的妇女无条件承担。这就是为什么发达资本主义国家反而注重家庭。而又之前对价值的非实体主义理解，没有进入交换也就不会产生价值，所以家务劳动不产生价值。**

# Heinrich: "Critique of Political Economy: An Introduction to Marx's 'Das Kapital'".
Heinrich: “Critique of Political Economy: An Introduction to Marx’s ‘Das Kapital’”. This thought movement started from Backhaus and was born out of the Frankfurt School. Adorno once said that Backhaus’s work was one of the most important works of the Frankfurt School. Whenever we talk about the Frankfurt School, we always think of some obscure cultural criticism and lack of political economy content. The New Marx Reading supplements this point. With “Das Kapital” as the core, they show the “political economy” dimension of the Frankfurt School.

Heinrich first talked about value. One of Heinrich’s important viewpoints is to oppose the substantialist understanding of value. When Marx described value in “Das Kapital”, he called it the undifferentiated human labor condensed in commodities, measured by socially necessary labor time. Here, value and use value seem to have a kind of Kantian dichotomy. Value seems like a kind of entity that is autonomously condensed in commodities by human labor. The problem is that commodities are not born as commodities, only when they are put into the market for exchange can they become commodities. Then the value of commodities is by no means determined at the time of production, but is determined in the exchange behavior in the market. Here Heinrich seems to explain value as a pure idea, but this is not the case. Commodities are produced, and only when they are recognized by the total socialized mass production, that is, entering the market for exchange, does it make sense to talk about their value. The problem here is the split between private labor and social mass production. The products produced by private labor will have no value if they are accumulated, and only when they are exchanged, and the value bestowed at that time is the part of private labor recognized by the total social labor. Heinrich also launched a critique of the labor theory of value in classical political economy. Ricardo started from the economic self-alienation, from the fact that commodities are dichotomized into value-things (imaginary things) and real things. He is committed to taking labor as the basis of value. He didn’t notice that after this work is done, the main thing is still not done. Because the fact that commodities separate themselves from themselves and transfer themselves into an independent kingdom of economic categories and are fixed as consciousness can only be explained by the self-splitting and self-contradiction of social labor. And money is the commodity that is produced in this exchange process for the purpose of exchange, the “king of commodities”.

In “Das Kapital”, the formation of money has experienced changes from individual value forms to expanded equivalent forms to general equivalent forms. Heinrich believes that the most important function of money is the measure of value. Because when serving as a measure of value, money participates in every process of capital appreciation as a persistent existence, that is, the well-known “G-W-G” formula process. Heinrich believes that fetishism is not so much a misrecognition of the relationship of exchange and production, but rather a correct recognition of the intermediary status of commodity exchange: under the condition of commodity production, producers are not directly and socially linked, but are mediated by their labor products. The relationship between people essentially appears as the relationship between things and things. In this relationship, the individual rationality is subordinate to the total irrationality of the system. Heinrich’s proposed solution to this is very simple: eliminate capitalism. Under the planned economic system, socialized mass production directly distributes private labor, cancels the intermediary of exchange, and thus cuts off the foundation of fetishism.

In this part, Heinrich (or the entire New Marx Reading) has a fatal problem: too much emphasis on the rigor of logic and neglecting the historical analysis. The entire analysis of the New Marx Reading is synchronic, it can only explain the logical inevitability of the simple production of capitalism transforming into capital appreciation, but not the conditions and history of the transformation. We know that the simple production of capitalism “W-G-W” does not transform into capital appreciation “G-W-G” in an instant. Take farmers as an example. In the feudal era, the production of farmers is first for their own use and secondly for taxation, and there is no logic of capitalist appreciation: the logic of commodity exchange, and what they carry out is the simple production of commodities. And only in the capitalist era do agricultural workers produce for the purpose of exchange. The difference here is that in the feudal era, because he is a farmer, he produces; while in the capitalist era, he chooses to become a farmer because he needs to engage in commodity exchange. We can see that the simple production of commodities and capital appreciation are not synchronous, but exist and transform depending on certain historical conditions.

Next, Heinrich criticized the traditional Marxist interpretation of Marx’s concept of exploitation. Traditional Marxists would think that exploitation is immoral, while Heinrich believes that exploitation is not “immoral” but “non-moral”. If we compare labor power to a tube of toothpaste, the capitalist actually pays the value of labor power when paying wages. And we also know that value and use value are not the same thing. For the capitalist, having bought this tube of toothpaste, of course he has to squeeze it clean. He will not only not think that squeezing the toothpaste is immoral, he will also complain that the toothpaste is not designed well and cannot be squeezed more cleanly. This is in line with the moral rights of the bourgeois legal system. But the proletariat also has something to say. Look how miserable the workers are. How can you squeeze people like this? Therefore, a antinomy appears here. Here, morality does not work, so it is non-moral. When morality fails, power plays a decisive role. Therefore, what determines the length of the working day is the struggle situation between workers and capitalists.

Heinrich also objects to the traditional interpretation of the concept of surplus value. Some scholars in the contemporary academic circle think that one can do without excess surplus value, as long as there is absolute and relative surplus value, or vice versa. Heinrich opposes this with a subjectless process. In Marx’s “Das Kapital”, the capitalist is to seek relative surplus value in order to improve productivity and extract more surplus value. Actually, if you think about it carefully, is the capitalist really that smart? Or, only capitalists who are familiar with the theory of surplus value will pursue increased productivity? Heinrich believes that relative surplus value is just the “leveled” excess surplus value. What makes the capitalist pursue technological progress and increased productivity is the compulsory competition. He must improve productivity, otherwise he will go bankrupt because he cannot get excess surplus value. Here is a subjectless process, and the operation of the structure itself will give its next stage. We don’t care whether the capitalist sees through this logic or not. In any case, he has to follow the operation of the structure unless he doesn’t want to be a capitalist anymore. This also leads to another assertion: it cannot be considered that the capitalist has an independent personality. The subject is only the recipient of the result of the operation of the structure, and the existence of the capital system needs to rely on their intermediary.

Finally, we have to discuss another issue that is of great political and academic significance: whether services, household chores and domestic work count as labor? Whether it is hired labor? Whether it is productive labor? Heinrich believes that these labors do not generate value. This is also the contradiction between Heinrich and feminist Marxists. Heinrich’s assertion here is actually a bit far-fetched, somewhat inferring from the result to the cause. He thinks that because the proletarian must be so poor that only freedom is left to enter the capitalist production system, and if household labor can generate value, then he is not so poor that only freedom is left, and he doesn’t have to enter the capitalist production system, so household labor does not generate value. And his premise “the proletarian must be so poor that only freedom is left to enter the capitalist production system” is completely true in Marx’s time, but not now. We can see that workers often do not enter the capitalist production system when they are desperate. For example, college graduates going to work in factories to screw bolts may not earn as much as they do live streaming. Some old capitalist countries that developed in the early last century will also advocate the view that labor is glorious, educating young people to enter factories and become hired workers. Or in some welfare countries, if young people are unemployed for a long time, many welfare benefits will be cancelled. If you are not a hired worker or a qualified labor force, you will not be recognized as a person. The state machinery has many ways to force the proletariat to enter the capitalist production system, so this premise is unnecessary. The factual situation is that household labor is the reproduction of labor power. This part cannot be put into the market of commodity exchange because the cost of labor power reproduction is very high and capital cannot afford it (at least for now). Therefore, this part must be unconditionally borne by women in the family. This is why developed capitalist countries pay more attention to the family. And according to the non-substantialist understanding of value before, if it does not enter the exchange, it will not generate value, so household labor does not generate value.
